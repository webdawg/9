The Whole Time

a story of the canopy, the ground, and the love that follows you down

The Whole Time

a picture book

For every kid who ever looked down and couldn't find the branch they came from — and for everyone waiting quietly above, watching the whole time.

High in the green, where the wind lives, Little Mo swung beside Big Koba every single morning.
They chased each other through leaves shaped like little green hands — and Koba always let Little Mo win. Almost always.
At night they curled into a nest as soft as a cloud, and Koba told stories about the stars that got stuck in the leaves.
One bright morning, while Koba dozed in the sun, Little Mo spotted a butterfly with wings like stained glass — and followed it just a little too far.
A branch cracked. The world spun green, then brown, then still.
Little Mo had never seen the ground before. It didn't sway. It didn't rustle. It just sat there, being enormous, and being the ground.
Little Mo looked up. The canopy was so far away it looked like a green sky. "I'm lost," Little Mo whispered. "The ground ate the whole world."
As the light dripped away, eyes bloomed in the dark like little yellow moons. Little Mo froze, heart pounding like a drum with no song.
A tangle of thorns grabbed at Little Mo's fur and would not let go, no matter how hard it pulled.
A slow, round shadow ambled closer. "Hold still," said the tortoise, and gently, patiently, worked the thorns loose one by one.
They hopped across the stream stone by stone, and for a moment, Little Mo almost forgot to be scared.
Then the sky rumbled. A vine bridge swayed over a deep, hungry ravine — the only way toward home.
Thunder cracked. The bridge bucked. Little Mo's small hands slipped — and the ground so far below opened its mouth.
Something in Little Mo's chest remembered Koba's voice: "I am always closer than you think." Little Mo looked up — really looked — and called out: "Koba!"
From the branch that had been there the entire time, a long strong arm reached down through the rain and caught Little Mo, just like that.
"You were there the whole time?" Little Mo asked. "The whole time," said Koba. "I never once looked away."
Now when Little Mo looks down at the ground, it isn't scary anymore. It's just the place that taught them how far a parent's love can reach.

The End
